Website Design Cost in Nigeria: A Realistic Breakdown

Website prices in Nigeria range hugely because 'a website' can mean very different things. Here's what actually drives the number up or down.

There is no single accurate answer to 'how much does a website cost in Nigeria' because the price depends entirely on what you're building — a five-page brochure site, a booking system, or a full online store. This guide breaks down the real factors that move price, without inventing numbers to make a point.

Overview

Website pricing in Nigeria is not standardised. Two companies quoting for what sounds like "the same website" can arrive at very different numbers because they're actually scoping different amounts of work — different page counts, different levels of custom design, different post-launch support, and different platforms.

Broadly, website projects in the Nigerian market fall into a few tiers: simple template-based sites at the lower end, custom-designed business websites in the middle, and full e-commerce or web application builds at the higher end. Where your project lands depends on complexity, not on which company you ask.

Some agencies publish starting prices publicly and structure payment in milestones — for example, a deposit, a payment at design approval, and a final payment at handover. That kind of transparency is worth looking for, since it makes it much easier to compare one quote against another honestly.

Why It Matters

Understanding what drives website cost protects you from two opposite mistakes: overpaying for a simple site because you didn't know what to ask for, or underpaying for a complex one and ending up with a rushed, unsupported build that needs to be redone within a year.

It also helps you have a more useful conversation with any company you're considering. Instead of just asking "how much," you can ask "how much for five custom pages with a contact form and basic SEO setup," which gets you a comparable, honest answer.

Benefits

Knowing the real cost drivers before you start shopping around gives you a real advantage:

  • You can budget accurately instead of being surprised by add-on costs partway through a project.
  • You can compare quotes properly by checking whether they cover the same scope, instead of just picking the lowest number.
  • You avoid overpaying for features or complexity your business doesn't actually need yet.
  • You avoid underpaying in a way that leaves you with a fragile site that breaks under real traffic or needs a rebuild soon after launch.
  • You can plan for future costs like hosting renewal, domain renewal, and ongoing support, which are often left out of the initial conversation.

How to Choose the Right Fit

Use this checklist to understand exactly what you're being quoted for before you compare prices:

  • Ask exactly how many pages are included, and what counts as 'a page' versus a section.
  • Ask if the design is fully custom or based on a pre-built template.
  • Confirm whether basic SEO setup (titles, meta descriptions, proper headings) is included or a separate cost.
  • Ask what happens after launch — is there a support window, and what's the cost to fix or update something after it ends?
  • Ask whether hosting and domain registration are included in the quote or billed separately.
  • If you need e-commerce, ask specifically whether payment gateway integration (like Paystack or Flutterwave) is included in the price.
  • Ask about the payment structure — a milestone-based schedule is generally safer than paying the full amount upfront.

Pricing in Nigeria

Rather than quoting a single figure that would quickly go stale, it's more useful to understand the factors that move price up or down:

  • Number of pages. A one-page or five-page brochure site costs less than a fifteen-page site with multiple service pages, team pages, and resources.
  • Custom design versus template. A fully custom design built around your brand takes more design time than adapting an existing template, and is priced accordingly.
  • Functionality. Contact forms, booking systems, membership logins, and especially e-commerce with payment integration all add development time.
  • Content readiness. If you need copywriting, professional photography, or a logo designed as part of the project, that adds cost on top of the build itself.
  • Support and maintenance. A project that includes a support period, backups, and future update assistance will be priced differently from a "build and walk away" project.
  • Timeline. Rush projects that compress a normal multi-week timeline into a few days often cost more due to the extra resourcing required.

Some Nigerian agencies, including Hoberg, publish transparent starting prices with a clear milestone payment structure rather than requiring a sales call just to get a rough number. When comparing quotes, always check what's actually included before assuming a lower number is the better deal.

Common Mistakes

These pricing-related mistakes come up repeatedly with Nigerian business owners building their first website.

1. Choosing based on price alone

The lowest quote is sometimes lowest because support, SEO setup, or mobile optimisation were left out entirely — costs that resurface later.

2. Not asking what happens after the free support period ends

Many quotes include a short support window, but ongoing fixes after that can carry separate, sometimes steep, hourly costs. Ask upfront.

3. Forgetting about hosting and domain renewal costs

These are recurring annual costs, separate from the one-time build cost. Factor them into your yearly budget, not just the initial project.

4. Paying the full amount before seeing any work

This removes your leverage if the project stalls or the final result doesn't match what was promised. A milestone structure protects you.

5. Assuming a higher price always means better quality

A high quote doesn't guarantee a good result any more than a low one guarantees a bad one. Judge based on portfolio and scope, not price alone.

Final Thoughts

Website pricing in Nigeria makes a lot more sense once you understand what's actually driving the number — pages, custom design, functionality, and support, not the company's name or how confidently they quote you a figure. Ask specific questions, compare scope against scope, and be wary of any price that seems disconnected from the actual work involved.
